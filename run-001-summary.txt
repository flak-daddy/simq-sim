3. YOUR BEHAVIOR: PATTERNS IN THE JOURNAL
------------------------------------------
- You wait when unsure. W1 open, W3 open, W4, W8 and W18 open were passes,
  usually because you wanted to "see which way the market goes" first. When
  you sold at midweek after waiting (W1, W3, W18), all three expired worthless.

- You lock in nearly-full profit early. Seven times you bought back an option
  for 0.01-0.03 at midweek, keeping 90-98% of the premium. In your words, there
  was "no need to risk about $2k worth of value over $1 of premium."

- You accept assignment rather than pay up to close. Both times a put went
  deep in the money by Wednesday (W2: ask 5.06 vs 0.65 received; W19: ask 3.09
  vs 0.31 received), you held and took the shares. You said you were "fine
  with riding the wheel back up the other side."

- You move strikes with the regime, especially late in the run. Put cushions
  were 4-7% below spot during the calm_up weeks and 13-18% below spot during
  the W20-W24 selloff and crash weeks. In the three crash weeks (W21-W23) you
  sold no puts at all, only calls.

- Your words and actions on "locking in a loss" don't match. Early on you said
  you didn't want to seal in the loss on assigned shares. In practice, most
  calls on both lots were struck below your purchase price: W7 C40 and W10 C48
  on a $52.50 lot, and C28-C37 on the $42.50 lot. The W10 call is the one that
  locked in the -$450.


4. STRENGTHS
------------
- Risk hygiene on winners. Buying back near-worthless options cost $32 in
  total and removed late-week gap risk on seven positions. That habit is cheap
  and sound.
- Patience. Waiting until midweek when the picture was unclear never cost you
  a loss in this run.
- Adapting to headlines. In W4 you said you didn't know how to read the
  headlines. By W20-W26 your trades lined up with the regime: no puts in crash
  weeks, far-OTM puts in selloffs, calls while holding shares in down weeks.
  That is a real improvement over the run.
- Consistency. You traded nearly every week, kept sizes fixed and never broke
  a rule. The books are clean.
- The strategy did its main job: it cut the loss from SimQ's 57% drop to 12%.


5. WEAKNESSES
-------------
- Small gains, big losses. In this run, put premium averaged under 1% a week,
  but each assignment cost several times that. The eight-week calm_up streak
  of expired puts earned about $307. The W19 assignment alone is down about
  $1,200 net of premium so far. A high win rate hid the fact that the
  strategy's total return was negative.

- Both assignments came from Monday-open puts sold in selloff regimes:
  W2 followed a selloff week, and in W19 the headlines pointed down. Both times
  the strike was within about one week's expected move of a 3x leveraged
  instrument (roughly 8-10% at those implied vols). Your later habit of
  widening the cushion in bad regimes came after both assignments.

- No exit plan for assigned shares. Holding to ride the wheel back up is a
  valid choice, but in a leveraged instrument that keeps trending down it
  means sitting on a growing loss while selling calls for $20-50 a week. There
  was no pre-set point where you would cut the position.

- Mixed signals on assigned stock. You resisted locking in the loss, but you
  repeatedly wrote calls below cost, which risks locking it in anyway, at
  whatever strike the call is at. Pick one approach per lot and apply it
  consistently.

- Idle cash during wheel cycle 2. With about $3,600 free during W20-W26, puts
  were sold in only three of seven weeks. In crash weeks that was the right
  call. It still meant interest did most of the work on that cash.

- Structural note (not a decision error): wheeling a 3x leveraged product
  exposes you to its volatility decay. Long stretches of chop or decline erode
  the shares faster than premium can rebuild them. Some of this run's result
  comes from the instrument itself, not from your choices.
