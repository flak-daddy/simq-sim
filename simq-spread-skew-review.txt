SIMQ SPREAD AND FAR-OTM PREMIUM REVIEW, PLUS REALISM RECOMMENDATIONS
===================================================================
Written: Mon 2026-10-26, after Run 5. Pricing v3 (skew and spreads from v2), market v3.
Question (user): "At times I felt the spreads were tough, with little premium left much
farther off the spot. Am I onto something, or is that just how it works?"
Script: tools/pricing-review/spread_skew_check.py (sections 1-5 below); the extra
midweek and stress-week numbers in section 4 came from a one-off run using the same
functions.
Real data: the Oct 23, 2026 TQQQ chains already in market-calibration.md (snapshots 1a
and 1b), plus the Oct 23, 2026 30-day skew readings from thetrading.tools.


SHORT ANSWER
------------
- Spreads: not supported. SimQ's spreads match the real TQQQ chain within a cent or two
  at every price level and cost you about 6% of your premium in Run 5. Real chains are
  worse than SimQ, not better, because SimQ never shows the erratic, much wider quotes
  real chains often have on .5 strikes and in-the-money options.
- Little premium far from spot: mostly just how weekly options on a 3x ETF work, with
  one real but small flaw. SimQ's put skew is a straight line, while real skew curves
  upward in the far wing, so far-OTM puts are a few vol points too cheap in SimQ. That
  is worth pennies per contract (about 5-15% more premium at 15-25% below spot), not
  dollars.
- What you felt was the spread's minimum width plus the steep drop-off in premium with
  distance. Both exist in real chains to the same or a greater degree.

2. PREMIUM FAR FROM SPOT
------------------------
Monday put bid as % of strike in SimQ (5-day, each regime's baseline implied vol):

  regime      5%     10%     15%     20%     25%     30%    first no-bid
  calm_up   1.26%   0.46%   0.13%   0.03%   0.00%   0.00%    24% below
  chop      2.35%   1.24%   0.61%   0.27%   0.11%   0.03%    35% below
  selloff   3.28%   2.06%   1.22%   0.69%   0.36%   0.17%    42% below
  crash     4.01%   2.72%   1.79%   1.12%   0.68%   0.39%    47% below
  rebound   3.44%   2.20%   1.34%   0.78%   0.43%   0.21%    44% below

In a calm week, each extra 5% of distance cuts the premium by roughly a factor of
three. That is the arithmetic of a weekly option, not a SimQ choice: at 54% implied vol,
a typical (one standard deviation) week moves about 7.6%, so a put 15% below spot is
two standard deviations away. Real TQQQ behaves the same way. The real 5-day chain on
Oct 23 had the 60 put (9.5% below) at about 92% vol, and SimQ's skew gives 92.1% for
that strike: an exact match.

You sold far out often. The median put in Runs 3-5 was 13% below spot, and 40% of
sales were 15% or more below. Your median bid was 0.85-0.95% of strike at 5-20% below
spot and 0.56% at 20-30% below. Those are higher than the calm row above because you
tended to go far out in volatile weeks, when premium is richer.


3. WHERE SIMQ IS GENUINELY THIN: THE FAR PUT WING
--------------------------------------------------
SimQ's skew is linear: each strike's IV = ATM IV x (1 + 1.3 x % below spot). That is
calibrated to the real chain out to about 10% below spot and matches it there. Beyond
that, real skew curves upward:
  - Real 30-day options, Oct 23: the 10-delta put traded at ATM + 29.9 vol points
    (at the 91st percentile of its history, so a fairly steep day). SimQ's linear skew
    gives ATM + 24.7 at the matching strike, about 27% below spot.
  - Real 1-day options, Oct 23: the gap between real and SimQ IV grows with distance,
    from 2.9 points at 3.5% below spot to 8.7 points at 11% below. Very short options
    have steeper skew per 1% of distance than weeklies, and SimQ uses the same slope for
    a 5-day Monday option and a 2-day Wednesday option.

How much this matters, with a candidate fix (add 1.0 x (% below spot)^2 to the put-side
skew, which matches the real 30-day 10-delta reading and leaves the 5-day 60 put at
92.9% vs real 92%):
  Monday bid % of strike, current -> candidate
    calm_up   15%: 0.13 -> 0.15    20%: 0.03 -> 0.04
    chop      15%: 0.61 -> 0.66    20%: 0.27 -> 0.31    25%: 0.11 -> 0.15
    selloff   15%: 1.22 -> 1.29    20%: 0.69 -> 0.78    25%: 0.36 -> 0.45
  On a $50 strike in a chop week, the 15% put goes from about $0.31 to $0.33.
  Blind Monday put edge (300 years): 10% below +0.37 -> +0.40%/week; 15% below
  +0.22 -> +0.27%; 20% below +0.14 -> +0.18%.

So you are onto something, but it's small. It would make far-out puts slightly better,
which favors your style.


4. RECOMMENDATIONS TO MAKE SIMQ MORE REALISTIC
----------------------------------------------
In order of how much they would change what you experience. Items 1-3 make the sim
harder (closer to real frictions); items 4-5 make far-OTM and midweek puts slightly
richer; items 6-8 are larger design choices.

1. Commissions. Charge $0.65 per contract on every open and close (assignment and
   call-away free, as at most brokers). Run 5 had 130 contract trades, so about $85, or
   2.4% of the premium collected. This is the largest real cost SimQ leaves out, and it
   hits the cent-or-two buy-backs hardest: a $0.01 buy-back really costs $1.65.

2. Messy quotes. Real weekly chains often show stale or much wider quotes, especially on
   .5 strikes and in-the-money options (snapshot 1b: 64 put quoted 1.80/2.20; 73.5 call
   0.16/0.71). Suggestion: each week, give a random
   ~20% of .5 strikes a spread 3-5x normal, and quote in-the-money options at 3x the
   normal width. Fills at a fair price would then take strike selection, as they do in
   real life.

3. Crash and selloff implied vol. SimQ prices stress weeks at 0.93-0.96x the volatility
   that actually happens; real TQQQ in May 2022 was about 0.81x. Trimming selloff/crash
   baselines by 8% (92 -> 85%, 105 -> 97%) cuts the blind Monday 10% put edge from
   +0.37% to +0.32%/week; by 12%, to +0.30%. Evidence is one real episode plus March
   2026 (where IV about equalled realized vol), so a modest trim (8%) is the defensible
   step.

4. Convex put wing (section 3). Add C x (% below spot)^2 to the put skew, with C around
   0.7-1.0. Small effect, real-world shape. Strikes within 10% of spot are unchanged.

5. Steeper skew for short expiries. Real 1-day skew is about 1.7x steeper per 1% than
   5-day skew. Scaling SimQ's slope by (5 / days left)^0.33 makes the Wednesday chain
   (2 days) about 1.36x steeper:
     Wednesday put bid % of strike, current -> steeper
       chop     10% below: 0.25 -> 0.30     selloff 10%: 0.54 -> 0.61
       crash    10% below: 0.81 -> 0.91     crash   15%: 0.32 -> 0.41
     Blind Wednesday put edge: 5% below +0.32 -> +0.37%/week; 10% below +0.10 -> +0.15%.
   You open about a quarter of your trades at midweek, so this one touches your play
   more than item 4 does. The 0.33 exponent comes from one 1-day chain; a real 2-day
   chain would pin it down.

6. Skew that moves with the market. Real skew steepens in stress: the 30-day 25-delta
   put-call gap went from 11 to 20 points in the month to Oct 23. SimQ uses one slope in
   every regime. Option: slope 1.1 in calm_up, 1.3 in chop and rebound, 1.5-1.6 in
   selloff and crash. The magnitudes need a real chain from a stressed day before
   adopting.

7. Less honest headlines. Today's headlines always describe the true regime. Real news
   is noisy and often wrong-footed. Option: 1 headline in 5 points the wrong way, or
   stays neutral during a regime change. This tests reading the market, not the sim.

8. Price path. SimQ is already harsher than TQQQ's 2010-2025 history (median year +8% vs
   +57%; drawdowns worse than -50% in 54% of years vs about 25%). That is a deliberate
   conservative choice, and I'd leave it. If you ever want a "bull market" run to
   compare, it should be a labeled market version, not the default.

Not recommended: widening spreads. The data in section 1 says they're already right.

Suggested bundle for a pricing v4 / chain v2: items 1, 2, 3 (8% trim), 4 (C = 0.8) and
5. Net effect on the blind Monday put (300 years, items 3 + 4 + commissions): 10% below
+0.37% -> +0.34%/week before commissions, +0.32% after one open and one close per week;
15% below +0.22% -> +0.22% before, +0.19% after. Easier far-OTM and midweek premium, harder execution
and crash weeks. That is closer to what real TQQQ put selling feels like.
