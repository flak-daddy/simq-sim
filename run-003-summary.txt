3. YOUR BEHAVIOR: PATTERNS IN THE JOURNAL
------------------------------------------
- Two-put Mondays carried into a selloff. The Run 2 habit of splitting nearly
  all cash across two strikes worked through W27, then W28's -22% selloff put
  both under water at once. The result was an assignment plus a -$242 close in
  the same week, and only ~$1,500 free afterwards.

- Two lots at once through a crash. After W30 you held 200 shares (costs $50
  and $33) with almost no free cash. That combination is what turned a bad
  stretch into a -48% drawdown: no premium to collect and no cash to sell puts
  at the lows.

- Calls below cost returned. W31 C34 on the $50 lot and W41 C31 on the $33 lot
  were both under purchase price. Neither was exercised, so it cost nothing
  here, but it breaks the "calls at or above cost" rule that worked in Run 2.

- Long full passes. W36-W40 had no positions at all. With SimQ at $23-29 and
  lot 4 bought at $33, near-cost calls had little or no bid, and the cash was
  too small for puts. You chose to wait rather than write calls far below cost.

- Smaller sizing after the drawdown. From W46 you ran one put at a time with
  half the cash, closer to spot (5-16% below) than your first-half Monday puts.
  Every one of them made money.

- Midweek roll-downs on calls (W44, W45) to collect a second premium. The W45
  roll to C34 was the one that got the shares called away, at a $100 profit.

5. WEAKNESSES
-------------
- Concentration in selloff stretches. Both second-half assignments (W28, W30)
  came in consecutive selloff weeks, and the second added a lot while the first
  was already down. Four selloff weeks in a row is rare, but in a 3x product a
  run like W28-W32 can take a lot from $50 to $18.

- Lot 3 was never worked after W31. At a $50 cost it needed SimQ to more than
  double from the lows before a call at cost had any bid. It finished the year
  $13.38 under water per share and accounts for the whole second-half loss.

- Idle capital. Ten weeks with no trade at all. The W36-W40 stretch was mostly
  forced (little cash, calls far below cost), but W48, W49 and W52 were passes
  with $5,200+ free.

- Two puts sized to nearly all cash leave no room to respond. Same note as Run 2,
  but this time it was tested and it hurt.


6. A NOTE ON THE SIM ITSELF
---------------------------
Regime labels and actual weekly price direction disagreed in 11 of the 41 weeks
that had a directional regime (calm_up, rebound, selloff, crash), and 8 of 23 in
the second half. Examples: W46 "rebound" -9.1%, W42 "selloff" +5.2%, W7
"calm_up" -14.7%. Selloffs were mostly right (9 of 11 down); calm_up was the
noisiest (7 of 19 down, though most of those were small). This may be intended
noise within a regime, but it makes TRUE headlines look wrong after the fact.
Worth a look in tools/pricing-review before Run 4 (how simq.py turns a regime
into weekly drift and volatility).


7. THINGS TO TRY IN RUN 4 (process, not strike picks)
-------------------------------------------------------

  while you already hold a lot. Closing it at midweek is the tool you already use.
- Go back to the Run 2 rule: covered calls at or above cost. Write down an
  exception rule in advance if you want one (for example, how far below cost).
- Track idle weeks with free cash separately from weeks where cash was locked.
- Keep the half-cash, one-put-at-a-time sizing from W46-W52 as a comparison
  against two-put Mondays.
