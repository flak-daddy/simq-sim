2. METHOD: A BLIND BASELINE
---------------------------
To separate the three causes, the same mechanical wheel was run on both runs' exact
price paths under each pricing version:
  - Every Monday, sell a put 10% (or 15%) below spot with all free cash.
  - Hold to Friday, with no midweek management.
  - If assigned, sell a covered call each Monday at the higher of the lot's cost or 5%
    above spot, held to Friday.
  - Cash earns 4%.
It never reads headlines, never closes early, and never changes its distance. Whatever
it earns is what the path and pricing hand out for free. The gap between it and you is
your contribution.

  10% put, pricing v3        -21.5%           +14.9%            +54.2%
  15% put, pricing v1         -3.4%            +9.8%            +21.5%

3. CAUSE 1: THE PATH (SEED AND MARKET MODEL), THE BIGGEST FACTOR
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Holding the rules and the pricing fixed (v3) and changing only the path:
  10% wheel: -21.5% on Run 1's path vs +14.9% on Run 5's path = 36 points
  15% wheel:  +2.7% on Run 1's path vs +17.8% on Run 5's path = 15 points

Run 1's path lost 57% in 26 weeks, with 11 selloff or crash weeks in a downward-drifting
market (v1). Run 5's path lost 21% in its first half, then rose 55% in the second. Part
of that is the seed and part is the market model: market v1's median year was about -55%
for SimQ, while market v2's is about +4% to +7%.

Most of the 38-point gap between Run 1 and Run 5 at Week 26 is here. A rule that never
thinks would have made 15-36 points more on Run 5's path than on Run 1's.

-----------------------------------------------
Run 1 (your -12.3%):
  Between the two blind rules: 14 points better than the 10% wheel (-26.3%) and 9 points
  worse than the 15% wheel (-3.4%), on v1 pricing.
  What cost you: puts 4-7% below spot in calm weeks, two assignments held into a falling
  market, and calls written below cost (the W10 C48 locked in -$450).

Run 5, first half (your +25.5%):
  8-11 points better than both blind rules (+14.9%, +17.8%). This is where your moves

Run 5, second half (your +21.2%):
  Behind the 10% wheel (+34.2% for the half) and level with the 15% wheel (+22.1%). In a
  rising market, the blind 10% rule kept all its cash in a put close to spot every week
  and got paid for it. You closed early, passed some Mondays, and often sold further out,
  which gave up premium in exchange for safety that this half didn't need.

Run 5, full year (your +52.1%):
  About the same as the blind 10% wheel (+54.2%) and 8 points ahead of the blind 15%
  wheel (+43.8%). Your process was better than Run 1's in identifiable ways, but on this
  path a rule that never looked at a headline did about as well.

Habit changes from Run 1 to Run 5 that did show up in results:
  - Cut losers at midweek (W2, W3, W38, W41) rather than riding into assignment.
  - Calls at or above cost on every lot; all four lots called away at a stock profit
    (Run 1: one lot called away at -$450, one held at -$1,497).
  - Put distance about 13% below spot on average (Run 1: 4-7% in calm weeks).


6. IS THE MODEL TILTED TOWARD PROFIT? YES, BY ABOUT +20% A YEAR
-------------------------------------------------------------
The +0.36% figure is not index drift. It is the blind put seller's average profit per
week (premium minus payouts, as % of collateral) for a Monday put 10% below spot under
market v2 and pricing v3. Compounded over 52 weeks it is roughly +20% a year, and the
Monte Carlo confirms it:

Blind wheel over 600 random 52-week years, pricing v3:
                     10% put: median   p10 / p90         beats cash   Run 5's +52.1% ranks
  Market v1               -25.1%       -72% / +52%          33%          90th percentile
  Market v2               +21.5%       -32% / +63%          69%          83rd percentile
  Market v3               +23.5%       -32% / +65%          71%          79th percentile
                     15% put: median
  Market v1               -14.7%       -70% / +45%          39%          95th percentile
  Market v2               +17.1%       -30% / +41%          72%          98th percentile
  Market v3               +20.3%       -25% / +42%          76%          96th percentile

So under the current model a mechanical put seller makes about +17-24% in a typical year
and beats cash about 70% of the time. Under market v1 the same rule lost money in a
typical year. That tilt is the variance risk premium in a market that drifts up: the
pricing charges about fair implied vol for each regime, but calm and rebound weeks (when
puts expire worthless) are now the common case.


7. ANSWERS
----------
Was it just the seed? Mostly the path, which is the seed plus the market model. Run 5's
path was a good year (SimQ +22% against a typical +4-7%), and the move from market v1 to
v2 turned a strategy that loses in a typical year into one that makes about +20%.

Was it the pricing? Partly. Pricing v3 pays several times what v1 did for out-of-the-money
puts, worth roughly 5-8 points over 26 weeks and about 20 points over Run 5's full year.

Did you make better moves? Yes, measurably in the first half: about 8-11 points ahead of
the blind rules, mostly from cutting losing puts early and managing lots well. Over the
full year your +52% matched the blind 10% wheel on the same path, so it is not evidence of
skill beyond what the model and this path handed out. Your process is clearly better than
Run 1's; the result is mostly the environment.

A fair way to judge future runs: compare your result to the blind 10% and 15% wheels on
the same path (the tool computes both), not to cash or buy-and-hold. In Run 5 you were
+8 to +11 points at Week 26 and -2 to +8 points at Week 52.

Caveats: one path per run is a small sample; the blind wheel puts all free cash in one
strike, which is more aggressive than your sizing; and Run 1's exact pricing mixed v1 and
v2, so its pricing comparison is approximate.
