SIMQ REALISM REPORT: HOW CLOSE IS SIMQ TO SELLING PUTS ON REAL TQQQ?
====================================================================
Written: Mon 2026-10-26, after Run 5. Model checked: market v3, pricing v3 (the Run 6 standard).
Scripts: tools/pricing-review/realism_internal.py (sections 1-3); blind-wheel Sharpe in
section 4 uses the wheel from tools/pricing-review/wheel_compare.py (600 years).
No user-supplied data; outside figures come from the public pages listed at the end.


SUMMARY
-------
- Option prices: realistic in calm weeks; a little generous to put sellers in selloff and
  crash weeks (SimQ's crash-week implied vol sits closer to realized vol than real TQQQ's
  did in May 2022).
- Price path: harsher than real TQQQ history. SimQ's typical year is +8% (real TQQQ
  2010-2025: about +57%), and deep drawdowns (worse than -50% in a year) are about twice as frequent.
- Payoff from selling puts: in line with real-world evidence. A blind put seller's
  risk-adjusted return in SimQ (Sharpe about 0.4) is a bit below the long-run S&P 500
  put-write indexes (0.5-0.67), and its advantage over buy-and-hold is about the same
  as theirs.
- Bottom line: the sim's profit tilt for put sellers is not an artifact. Real index
  put-writing has earned a similar risk-adjusted premium for decades. The main places
  SimQ is kinder than reality are execution (perfect fills, no costs) and crash-week
  option prices; the main place it is harsher is the price path itself.

  Drawdowns of 40% or worse      most years (median in-year  7 episodes in 16 years
                                 drawdown -52%)              (2010, 2011, 2015-16, 2018,

                                                             just missed at -69.9%)
  Weekly moves                   sd 10.1%; 5.8 weeks a year  (not checked; no free
                                 below -10%, 1.5 below -20%  weekly data)

Leverage mechanics check: in simulated years when the unleveraged index ended within
+/-3%, SimQ ended a median -20%. That is volatility decay plus the 0.03%/day cost
(about 7.6%/yr, close to TQQQ's expense ratio plus its cost of leverage). Real 3x funds
behave this way.

Verdict: SimQ's typical year is deliberately pessimistic. Real TQQQ's history happened
during one of the strongest Nasdaq runs on record (QQQ +19%/yr since 2010), and the
calibration notes set SimQ well below it on purpose. SimQ also has deep drawdowns about
twice as often as real TQQQ did. For a put seller that is a tougher environment than
history, not an easier one.


2. OPTION PRICES: IMPLIED VS. REALIZED VOLATILITY
------------------------------------------------
                  SimQ realized   SimQ implied (v3)   Ratio     Real TQQQ ratio
  calm_up               43%             54%           1.25x     1.12x (Aug 2023, 30-day IV
                                                                vs 20-day HV); about 1.3x
                                                                (Oct 2026 weekly chain)
  chop                  62%             75%           1.21x
  rebound               87%             95%           1.09x
  selloff               95%             92%           0.96x     0.81x (May 2022, 30-day IV
  crash                113%            105%           0.93x     101% vs 20-day HV 125%)

Calm weeks: SimQ's 1.25x sits between real 30-day options (1.12x) and real weekly
options (about 1.3x). Realistic.
Stress weeks: SimQ prices crash and selloff options at 0.93-0.96x their realized vol;
real TQQQ options in May 2022 traded at about 0.81x (30-day IV against a trailing 20-day
realized vol, so this is an approximate comparison). SimQ therefore pays sellers somewhat
more in bad weeks than the real market did in that episode.
Calm-week realized vol is also lower in SimQ (43%) than in the real calm stretch checked
(Aug 2023 20-day HV averaged 51%).

Internal consistency: put-call parity holds on all 1,647 mid prices tested, and put prices
rise and stay convex across strikes (no arbitrage).


3. HOW FRAGILE IS THE PUT-SELLER EDGE?
-------------------------------------
Blind Monday put 10% below spot, held to Friday (premium at the bid minus payout, % of
collateral per week, 300 simulated years):
  Pricing v3 as is ................. +0.37%/week (about +19%/yr)
    by regime: calm_up +0.84%, chop +0.74%, rebound +1.79%, selloff -2.51%, crash -4.58%
  All implied vols x0.95 ........... +0.22%
  All implied vols x0.90 ........... +0.08%
  All implied vols x0.85 ........... -0.05%
  Calm/chop implied vols only x0.80  -0.01%

So the whole edge disappears if SimQ's option prices are about 13% too high across the
board, or about 20% too high in calm and chop weeks alone. The real-data checks in
section 2 suggest calm weeks are about right and stress weeks are a bit rich; correcting
only the stress weeks to the May 2022 ratio would trim the edge but not erase it, since
most of it is earned in calm, chop and rebound weeks.


4. DOES SELLING PUTS PAY IN THE REAL WORLD?
------------------------------------------
Real cash-secured put-writing indexes on the S&P 500 (gross of costs):
  PUT (monthly, at the money), Jul 1986-Dec 2015: 10.1%/yr vs S&P 500 9.8%, with
    volatility 10.1% vs 15.3%; Sharpe 0.67 vs 0.47.
  PUT, Feb 2006-Dec 2015: 6.6%/yr, Sharpe 0.52, max drawdown -33% (S&P 7.1%, 0.46, -51%).
  WPUT (weekly), Feb 2006-Dec 2015: 5.6%/yr, Sharpe 0.50, max drawdown -24%.
  PUT since Jan 2007: 7.2%/yr, volatility 10.7%, max drawdown -32.7% (S&P 11.0%, 15.4%,
    -50.9%).
  Bondarenko's study for Cboe: S&P 500 options were richly priced in every year since
    1990 except 2008.

The same comparison in SimQ (blind wheel, 600 simulated years, market v3, pricing v3):
                          Mean year   Volatility   Sharpe (vs 4% cash)
  Blind 10% put wheel      +18.5%       34.3%        0.42
  Blind 15% put wheel      +13.9%       26.8%        0.37
  SimQ buy-and-hold        +38-42%     113%          0.30-0.34

Two things line up with the real record:
  - Put writing beats buy-and-hold on a risk-adjusted basis by a similar margin
    (SimQ 0.37-0.42 vs 0.30-0.34; S&P 0.50-0.67 vs 0.46-0.47).
  - The put writer's Sharpe in SimQ (about 0.4) is a little lower than real index
    put-writing (0.5-0.67), not higher.
So the profit you see from selling puts in SimQ is the same volatility risk premium that
real put writers have collected for decades, sized for a 3x product. It is not something
the model invents.

What this can't tell you: no free source publishes a long backtest of weekly puts on TQQQ
itself, so the TQQQ-specific premium is inferred from TQQQ's implied/realized vol figures
(section 2) and from S&P put-writing, not measured directly.


5. WHAT SIMQ SIMPLIFIES
-----------------------
Kinder than reality:
  - Fills always at the quoted bid/ask; no stale, missing or erratic quotes.
  - No commissions or fees. At about $0.65 per contract, a $20-$50 premium loses 1-3%.
  - No early assignment, taxes, or margin rules.
  - Headlines are honest by construction (reported data is always true and regimes
    exist). Real news has no hidden regime behind it.
Harsher than reality:
  - Lower typical return and more frequent deep drawdowns than TQQQ's actual history
    (section 1).
  - The biggest single days look heavier than real TQQQ in a typical year (SimQ median
    worst day -17.3%; 38 days a year move more than 5%). Not verified, because no free
    source lists TQQQ's worst day per year.
Neutral:
  - Volatility decay and leverage cost behave like a real 3x fund (section 1).
