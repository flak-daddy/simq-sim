3. YOUR BEHAVIOR: PATTERNS IN THE JOURNAL
------------------------------------------
- Laddered puts close to spot after big drops. From W32 on you often sold three
  puts at adjacent strikes ($1 apart) a few percent to 15% below spot. In calm or
  rebound weeks this collected well (W33, W41, W44). In a down week the whole
  ladder went under together: W45 put two lots on at once.

- Averaging down into a falling 3x product. After lot 2 ($25) you kept selling
  puts at lower and lower strikes and took assignment at $12, $13, $10.50 and
  $9.50. Each new lot was cheaper, but SimQ kept falling, so the lots added more
  share exposure rather than offsetting the first one. By year end 73% of the
  account was in shares.

- Calls only on the newest lot. You held to the "calls at or above cost" rule
  from the Run 3 review: C12 on the $12 lot, C13 on the $13 lot, C10.5 on the
  $10.50 lot. That rule meant lots 2, 3 and 4 had little or no bid at cost once
  SimQ fell under $10, so they sat uncovered for weeks.

- Midweek management stayed sharp. 26 midweek buy-backs over the year, 20 of them
  at 0.01-0.02. W44's close-all-three was a clean example.

- Sizing moved toward the Run 3 advice and then away from it. In W46-W52 you
  kept roughly a third to two thirds of free cash in one put, but W45's three-put
  Monday ($3,600 of $5,805) came right before the assignments.

- Fewer idle weeks than Run 3 (6 versus 10), and most passes were Monday passes
  followed by midweek trades.


4. STRENGTHS

5. WEAKNESSES
-------------
- Share exposure kept growing as SimQ fell. Six assignments, five of them in the
  second half, with no lot closed after W9. In a 3x product that halved twice in
  six months, every lot lost money on paper.

- Lot 2 ($25, W30) was never worked. At year end it needs SimQ to nearly triple
  before a call at cost has a bid. It alone accounts for -$1,622.

- Puts sized and placed close to spot during selloff stretches. W30 (two puts
  17-20% out on a -31% week) and W45 (three puts 5-20% out before a -11% Thursday)
  were the two biggest second-half hits.

- Lost to cash for the year. Premium income (+$751 net) plus interest was not
  enough to cover marks on lots bought during a long decline.


6. A NOTE ON THE SIM ITSELF
---------------------------

7. THINGS TO TRY IN RUN 5 (process, not strike picks)
-------------------------------------------------------
- Set a share-exposure cap in advance (for example, no new puts once a set share
  of the account is already in lots), and decide what you do when you hit it.
- Decide an exception rule for calls on deep under-water lots before the year
  starts (for example, how far below cost you'd accept), or accept that those lots
  will sit idle.
- When laddering several puts, consider how many assignments the ladder could
  produce in one bad week and whether you want that many lots.
- Keep tracking idle weeks; Run 4's six is the lowest so far.
