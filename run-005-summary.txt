3. YOUR BEHAVIOR: PATTERNS IN THE JOURNAL
------------------------------------------
- Close-and-roll at midweek became the default. 45 buy-backs, most of them at a
  cent or two, usually followed by a new put closer to spot for the last two days.
  The midweek re-sales collected $11-$135 each and kept the cash working.

- Losers were cut early. W2, W3, W38 and W41 puts were bought back at midweek once
  they went near or into the money, instead of riding them to assignment. The
  four assignments all came from puts you held through Friday.

- Calls stayed at or above cost, with one reroute. In W13 you moved an in-the-money
  C35 from lot 1 ($38 basis) to lot 2 ($33.72 basis) before Friday, so the
  call-away produced a gain instead of a loss. All four lots were called away
  within one to twelve weeks.

- Share exposure stayed small. At most two lots at once (W12) and never more than
  one lot after W13. This is the biggest change from Run 4's six assignments and
  five held lots.

- Sizing: early in the year single puts used most of the free cash (W2 P55 7%
  below spot; W3 P42 20% below, then P40 3% below at midweek), and that is where
  the -23% drawdown came from. From W16 on the losing trades stayed small (largest
  -$252).

- Slips caught before fills: a "call" that would have been called below cost
  (W7, you meant put), a non-listed strike (W21 P41.5), a same-strike roll that
  would have cost the spread (W45), and "68/5" read as 68.5 (W51).

5. WEAKNESSES
-------------
- The first five weeks. Puts sold into the W2 selloff and W3 crash (P55, P42,
  P40) cost -$465 net on buy-backs plus the lot 1 assignment at $40 with SimQ at $31.
  The account didn't regain $7,500 until W14.

- Lagged buy-and-hold in the second half (+21% against +55%). With SimQ rising
  most weeks, cash-secured puts capture only the premium, not the move. That is
  inherent to the strategy, not a mistake, but it is the cost of the protection
  the first half needed.

- Midweek puts sold close to spot in volatile weeks could still go wrong late:
  W34's P62.5 (sold 8.6% below spot midweek) was assigned after a -15% Friday.

- One expensive roll: the W37 C67.5 buy-back cost $252, although the replacement
  C70 recovered it and more.


6. A NOTE ON THE SIM ITSELF
---------------------------
First run on market v2. SimQ finished +22% for the year, above the design median
(about +4%) but well inside the v2 range (10th/90th percentile -60% / +155%).
The regime mix (calm_up 50%, chop 15%, rebound 15%, selloff 10%, crash 10%) had
more crashes than the v2 average (5 against about 1.7 per year), including three
in a row (W3-W5): two crash -> crash transitions at 0.10 each under v2.

Regime and weekly direction disagreed in 11 of 44 directional weeks (5 of 22 in
the second half), e.g. W12 calm_up -10.8% and W48 calm_up -10.9%. Per
market-calibration.md this is by design (regime sets drift and volatility, not
the sign of each week).
